How it was made

The lists

Each map follows one list from the acts of the council: the signatures to its decree or canons where they survive, the attendance and signature lists at Ephesus, the bishops present at the first session in 879, and the list of participants at Lateran IV. Emperors, officials, abbots and envoys are left out; presbyters and deacons who signed for a bishop are kept.

Sees, not birthplaces

A bishop signed for his see, and only a handful of birthplaces are recorded, so each name is mapped at the see it stood for. A bishop who came to several councils appears once in each list, so the totals count names, not people.

Placing the sees

Sees are identified from the editions and their indexes, and placed with the Pleiades gazetteer, the Barrington Atlas and, for the Latin West, GeoNames. A see named in the source and located is drawn as a dot; one identified or located only by modern scholars is starred in the lists; a see that cannot be located is placed in its province with a dashed ring; a see that cannot be identified is listed but not mapped.

Present-day countries

The country is that of the site today, from Natural Earth. Crimea is counted with Ukraine. The Golan Heights are counted separately. The regions in the chart group these countries: Turkey; Greece and Cyprus; the Balkans, the Black Sea and the Caucasus; the Levant and Iraq; Egypt and North Africa; Italy; the rest of Europe.
